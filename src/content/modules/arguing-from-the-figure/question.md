In the triangle $ABC$ the base $AB$ has length $1$, and the angles at $A$ and
$B$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$, where $0 < \alpha \leq \beta$. Points $P$ on
$AC$ and $Q$ on $BC$ satisfy $AP = PQ = QB = x$, and $PQ$ makes an angle
$\theta$ with the line through $P$ parallel to $AB$.

**(i)** Show that $x\cos\theta = 1 - x\cos\alpha - x\cos\beta$, find
$x\sin\theta$ in terms of $x$, $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and deduce that

$$
\bigl(1 + 2\cos(\alpha+\beta)\bigr)x^{2} - 2(\cos\alpha + \cos\beta)\,x + 1 = 0. \tag{$*$}
$$

Show that $(*)$ still holds if $P$ lies on $AC$ produced and $Q$ on $BC$
produced. ($P$ lies on $AC$ produced if it is on the line through $A$ and $C$
with the points in the order $A$, $C$, $P$.)

**(ii)** State the condition on $\alpha$ and $\beta$ under which $(*)$ is
linear in $x$. When that condition fails, with $0 < \alpha \leq \beta$ still,
show that $(*)$ has two distinct real roots.

**(iii)** Find the possible values of $x$ when (a) $\alpha = \beta = 45^\circ$
and (b) $\alpha = 30^\circ$, $\beta = 90^\circ$, and sketch each case.
